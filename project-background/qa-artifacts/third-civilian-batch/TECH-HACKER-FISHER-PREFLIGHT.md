## 1. Tech — field systems technician

**Role/personality:** resourceful, precise, absorbed in practical problem-solving; a hands-on maintainer who repairs the city's physical systems rather than a medical worker or abstract office analyst.

### Proposed silhouette
- **Head:** short swept-back undercut with a small side-part ridge and compact ear-protection/comm cup. No brim, projecting bill, visor or face-obscuring goggles; eyes and brow remain visible.
- **Torso:** compact high-collar work shell with an offset cross-body equipment harness and a short, split rear utility flap. The harness is structural and follows the torso rather than reading as a Courier messenger bag.
- **Arms/hands:** one forearm is brought inward to inspect a small palm-sized diagnostic meter; the opposite arm hangs free or braces against the harness. The meter must be supported by a visible hand and wrist.
- **Legs/stance:** slight forward lean, one knee flexed and one foot set outward as if working at a service panel. Keep the feet grounded; not a running/athletic pose.
- **Rear:** harness crossing, compact cable/tool loops and short flap create a clear rear rhythm. No medical yoke or pouch.

### Face identity
- Front: narrow focused eyes under a level brow; slightly longer lower face with a small, compressed mouth.
- Profile: angular nose bridge and a firmer chin; swept-back hairline and ear cup remain separate shapes.
- Closest-face comparisons to validate against current roster: Coach (eye/brow region) and Courier (lower-face taper). Tech should have a more level brow and longer, squarer chin than Coach, and a less tapered cheek/jaw than Courier.

### Exactly three proposed must-survive items
1. Swept-back undercut plus visible compact ear-protection cup (**direct 28×42 head-read cue**).
2. Short high-collar field shell with offset crossing equipment harness and split rear flap.
3. Inward-held diagnostic meter with a visible supporting hand/wrist; if the meter collapses at 28×42, preserve the hand-to-tool interaction as the read.

**Allowed to simplify/disappear at 28×42:** tiny meter buttons, cable loops, harness buckles, stitching and ear-cup seams.

**Prop/contact plan:** meter held close to the torso in character-left hand (front: viewer-right); character-right arm remains free. Record near/far occlusion separately in both profiles and ensure the rear harness does not imply a second floating meter/hand. If the meter makes the pose unreadable, remove it rather than add a fourth identity cue.

**Palette direction:** deep petrol/blue-green work fabric, muted graphite structural planes, warm copper/amber instrument accent and a small pale neutral face/harness separation. Avoid Clinic teal/sea-glass and Coach forest/cream. Colour is subordinate to the shell and stance.

**Face/head check:** face remains open and legible beneath hair/ear protection at native 64×96 and direct 28×42. Do not let the ear cup cover the profile eye.

## 2. Hacker — independent digital operator

**Role/personality:** guarded, clever, self-contained and quick; more improvisational and evasive than Tech. A digital operator, not a uniformed infrastructure technician.

### Proposed silhouette
- **Head:** uneven side-swept fringe with one temple exposed and a small asymmetrical earbud; no cap, helmet or Tech-like ear-protection cup. The hood is optional and should not be the primary identity.
- **Torso:** cropped, asymmetric drape-shell/short mantle over a close-fitting base layer. One side has a short hanging panel; the other exposes the forearm and a strong waist break. Avoid a generic symmetrical hoodie.
- **Arms/hands:** one elbow tucked close while the hand manipulates a slim fold-out input slate at waist height; the other hand is relaxed and partially hidden by the drape. Avoid Tech's two-handed inspection pose.
- **Legs/stance:** narrow, slightly folded stance with one toe turned inward and weight carried off-centre. Keep a clear gap between the legs; no athletic crouch or Courier stride.
- **Rear:** asymmetric mantle panel and exposed shoulder/hood seam provide a recognisable rear contour. Do not add a large backpack.

### Face identity
- Front: one brow sits lower, one eye slightly narrowed; a small off-centre mouth gives a knowing, guarded expression.
- Profile: short upturned nose and compact pointed chin; fringe creates a distinct forehead break without covering the visible eye.
- Closest-face comparisons to validate against current roster: Vendor (cheek/expression) and Referee (brow/eye placement). Hacker should have a narrower lower face and more asymmetrical brow line than Vendor, and a sharper chin/less open expression than Referee.

### Exactly three proposed must-survive items
1. Side-swept uneven fringe with exposed temple and small single earbud (**direct 28×42 head-read cue**).
2. Cropped asymmetric drape-shell with one hanging side panel and an exposed opposite forearm.
3. Tucked-in one-hand fold-out input-slate interaction that remains visibly connected to wrist/hand.

**Allowed to simplify/disappear at 28×42:** tiny slate keys, cable/earbud wire, small seam marks and fine panel stitching.

**Prop/contact plan:** slate held low in character-right hand (front: viewer-left); character-left arm is tucked/partly occluded by the drape. In profiles, show the near wrist and slate edge only when physically supported; far-side hand stays occluded. Rear view must not duplicate the slate or invent a second hand.

**Palette direction:** muted aubergine/ink plum, charcoal-black shadow, cool dusty lilac fabric plane and a small electric-lime or acid-cyan device accent. Avoid Guard graphite/ochre, Vendor berry/saffron and Tech petrol/copper. Do not rely on neon to establish the role.

**Face/head check:** exposed temple and eye must remain visible in both profiles; the hood, if retained, is a secondary drape not a face mask. Avoid an anonymous blank dark face.

## 3. Fisher — waterfront net worker

**Role/personality:** weathered, patient, practical and quietly resilient; a working waterfront civilian rather than a sailor, adventurer or generic tradesperson.

### Proposed silhouette
- **Head:** low oilskin rain hood/cowl with a short forward brow lip and a soft rear neck flap; leave the face open. No newsboy cap or naval cap. Hair is mostly tucked away, with a small nape tuft visible from behind.
- **Torso:** broad, low-set waterproof smock with a bib-like chest plane and a diagonal shoulder closure. Keep the hem short enough to expose substantial trousers and boots; do not copy Cook's apron wedge.
- **Arms/hands:** one arm hangs low holding a compact folded net basket by its upper rim; the other rests against the hip or grips a short net float line. The prop stays below the waist and must not obscure the torso.
- **Legs/stance:** wide braced stance with one knee flexed and heavy deck boots; weight leans subtly into the wind. This should be a stable work stance, not Guard's squared defensive brace.
- **Rear:** broad hood flap, shoulder seam and a small net coil/float attachment make the back recognisable. Avoid Sailor's cap-led silhouette or a large backpack.

### Face identity
- Front: broad weathered cheek planes, low relaxed brow and a small downturned mouth; eyes slightly deep-set.
- Profile: blunt nose tip, stronger lower lip and a rounded, sturdy chin; hood edge remains behind the brow/eye line.
- Closest-face comparisons to validate against current roster: Guard (jaw mass) and Cook (cheek volume). Fisher should have a softer jaw corner than Guard and a longer, more weathered cheek-to-chin plane than Cook.

### Exactly three proposed must-survive items
1. Low oilskin hood with forward brow lip and rear neck flap (**direct 28×42 head-read cue**).
2. Broad short waterproof smock with diagonal bib closure and exposed heavy trouser/boot break.
3. Low-held compact net basket/float interaction with a visible supporting hand; if the net is too noisy at 28×42, retain a simple net rim and grip rather than adding fine mesh.

**Allowed to simplify/disappear at 28×42:** individual mesh holes, float knots, seam stitching, tiny toggles and small water marks.

**Prop/contact plan:** compact net basket held in character-left hand (front: viewer-right), below waist level. Character-right hand rests near the hip/float line. Profiles must show one near-side grip only; far-side and rear views use occlusion, not mirrored duplicate basket rims or hands. Keep all prop geometry inside the 64×96 sprite field.

**Palette direction:** muted storm-teal/oilskin green, dark marine charcoal, weathered canvas/salt-grey and a restrained rust-orange float accent. Avoid Clinic teal/sea-glass, Tech petrol/copper and Sailor's likely navy/ivory direction. Large value planes must remain distinct at direct game scale.

**Face/head check:** hood must frame, not hide, the face. The brow, eye, nose and chin must remain visible in both profiles; do not solve the role with a blank hood silhouette.
