## 1. Guard — security / protective role

**Legacy clues, not a template:** helmet-style head, guard torso, baton in front/side and rear guard plate.

**Identity objective:** capable security/protection worker, not Usher in darker colours or Coach with a different cap.

**Silhouette exploration:** test protective/segmented outerwear and a grounded stance; avoid a long formal coat or athletic warm-up jacket. Make helmet/head protection structurally distinct from Usher's peaked cap, Coach's flat cap and Referee's cap. Explore distinctive shoulder/torso/leg relationships and negative spaces; baton/gear must not be the only role cue.

**Face/head questions:** how much face is visible beneath the helmet; define brow/eye and jaw/nose construction for front/profile; make rear helmet/nape intentional.

**Candidate must-survive trio (finalize after silhouette tests):** protective head/shoulder contour; role prop or integrated protective-gear interaction if legible at 28×42; strong uniform/gear value block.

**Prop planning:** legacy baton is optional. If retained, document anatomical hand, front/rear viewer-side, both profiles, wrist/hand contact and occlusion. Do not assume legacy “right hand” is correct.

## 2. Vendor — market / stall worker

**Legacy clues, not a template:** hat-style head, bowl in front and apron ties in profile/rear. Cook shares this old vendor archetype family.

**Identity objective:** market/service vendor, visibly unlike Cook; not a chef recolour.

**Silhouette exploration:** avoid Cook's broad chef-coat/apron-front wedge and toque/curl mass. Explore different torso/garment logic (e.g. layered vest, overshirt, waist apron or utility sash), selected only after four-view silhouette tests. Give headwear its own contour; no chef hat. Consider customer-facing trading posture, not a pan-presenting pose.

**Face/head questions:** define two construction cues that distinguish Vendor from Cook and closest market/service faces; expression must read without relying on the hat.

**Candidate must-survive trio (finalize after silhouette tests):** distinctive non-chef headwear/head shape; market prop/customer interaction if legible at 28×42; garment/torso construction unlike Cook's apron wedge.

**Prop planning:** legacy bowl is optional. If retained, decide what it communicates and show clear grip/support; do not let it become an unreadable blob.

## 3. Clinic worker — healthcare role

**Legacy clues, not a template:** hair-band head, clinic torso, medical kit in front/side and rear yoke. Tech and Hacker share this legacy archetype family.

**Identity objective:** healthcare worker, not generic coat-wearing civilian, Tech, Hacker or Cook.

**Silhouette exploration:** avoid generic long lab coat or copying Usher's long formal coat. Explore clinical-work garment with distinct length, closure, collar and lower-body relationship. Give hair/head construction deliberate rear/profile shapes; do not rely on hair band alone. Use calm, attentive posture; avoid Coach's clipboard stance or Cook's pan pose.

**Face/head questions:** define two purposeful face-construction cues and a direct-scale head-read cue; compare against Cook, Usher, Coach and future Tech/Hacker briefs when available.

**Candidate must-survive trio (finalize after silhouette tests):** distinct clinical head/hair construction visible at 28×42; medical-kit/care interaction if clearly integrated; distinct clinical garment/torso construction and value block.

**Prop planning:** legacy medical kit is optional. If retained, define carrying hand, handle/contact, occlusion and mirrored profiles.
