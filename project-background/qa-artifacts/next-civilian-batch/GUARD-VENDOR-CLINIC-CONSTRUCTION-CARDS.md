# Guard, Vendor and Clinic Worker — silhouette construction cards

**Status:** first design exploration; owner silhouette review required  
**Scope:** design only. No live renderer/game code, production colour art, snapshots or approval states changed.  
**Scale convention:** each figure is designed in a 64×96 construction field. The companion SVG presents matched-scale four-view silhouettes. Profiles are mirrored intentionally for left/right presentation; the body construction remains character-relative.

## Design rationale and roster separation

These are three distinct garment/head/pose systems. They deliberately avoid the six existing constructions: Usher's long formal coat, Cook's chef/apron mass, Courier's flared messenger coat, Runner's airborne singlet, Coach's straight-hem warm-up jacket/yoke and Referee's striped official shirt/shorts.

### 1. Guard — security / protective role

**Personality:** observant, dependable, quietly authoritative; protective rather than aggressive.

**Silhouette construction**
- Low protective helmet with a short brow shelf and rear nape guard; face remains exposed beneath the brow.
- Squared shoulder protection over a compact overshirt, with a clearly broken waist/belt line and short split lower edge.
- Forearms are kept outside the torso contour. A single bracer plane gives the near arm a protective read without using crossed arms.
- Stable, slightly wide planted stance; substantial work boots with separated legs.
- Rear view carries a shaped helmet nape and central back protection rather than a blank jacket rectangle.

**Face construction cues**
- Front: heavier, straighter brow shelf; close-set eye placement; broad lower-jaw silhouette with compact chin.
- Profile: firmer brow-to-nose projection and a stronger jaw/chin corner than Vendor or Clinic Worker.
- Back: helmet shell and nape contour are the head-read; no face marks.

**Exactly three must-survive items**
1. Low helmet brow/nape contour and squared shoulder silhouette (**28×42 head-read cue: helmet brow plus rear nape profile**).
2. Compact protective overshirt with belt break and short split hem.
3. Integrated protective bracer/radio equipment relationship; role must remain readable without a baton.

**Prop/contact and handedness**
- Primary proposal: compact radio clipped to the upper chest/belt; it is integrated equipment, not a handheld prop.
- Optional baton is intentionally omitted from this first silhouette. If added after review, show one continuous shoulder → upper arm → forearm → wrist → hand chain, explicit grip and overlap, and the same character-relative hand in all directions.
- Front: character-left maps to viewer-right. Rear: character-left maps to viewer-left. Profiles: identify near/far arm relative to facing. The bracer is on character-right; verify its appearance in each profile before renderer production.

**28×42 simplification**
- Preserve helmet shelf, shoulder corners, belt break, boot separation.
- Tiny radio buttons, rivets, seam divisions and fine belt hardware may disappear.

**Closest roster comparisons to perform in the owner review**
- Usher: confirm Guard's short split protective shell is not a dark long-coat column.
- Coach: confirm squared protective shoulder/helmet geometry does not become a cap-and-yoke athletic jacket.
- Referee: confirm protective segmented torso is not read as striped official shirt/shorts.

**Open design question:** whether the forearm bracer is a strong enough secondary shape in rear/profile views without adding a handheld prop.

### 2. Vendor — market / stall worker

**Personality:** approachable, quick-witted, practical and welcoming; confident with regular customers.

**Silhouette construction**
- Soft market cap with a modest sloped brim and slightly irregular crown; no toque or large curl mass.
- Open short waistcoat over rolled sleeves. The central opening leaves a distinct shirt/torso negative space.
- Narrow, offset sash/apron panel rather than a broad chef-apron wedge.
- Slight forward/customer-facing posture with one arm bent toward a shallow tray held close to the waist; the other arm remains relaxed.
- Rear waistcoat seam and tied sash ends create a different rhythm from Cook's apron panel.

**Face construction cues**
- Front: rounded cheek mass, lifted outer brow and asymmetrical friendly smile.
- Profile: softer nose slope and fuller cheek-to-chin transition; less angular jaw than Guard and a gentler chin than Clinic Worker.
- Back: cap crown and fold create a low, sloped head contour.

**Exactly three must-survive items**
1. Sloped soft market cap plus rounded cheek contour (**28×42 head-read cue: cap brim/crown and cheek mass**).
2. Open waistcoat over rolled sleeves with a narrow offset sash/apron; never a broad apron wedge.
3. Compact shallow market tray/bundle interaction held at waist level with visible hand support.

**Prop/contact and handedness**
- First exploration uses a shallow tray held close to the body, supported by the character-left hand (viewer-right in front; viewer-left in rear).
- The support hand sits visibly beneath the tray lip; fingers/palm overlap its underside. The opposite arm bends toward the tray rim without hiding the wrist.
- Profiles must retain the shallow tray edge and hand-under-tray relationship; avoid a bowl/pan profile that merges with the torso.
- The tray is a market object, not Cook's handled pan. No handle is present in this design.

**28×42 simplification**
- Preserve cap slope, open vest negative space, narrow sash and tray/hand block.
- Buttons, individual produce pieces, fine apron stitching and cloth folds may disappear.

**Closest roster comparisons to perform in the owner review**
- Cook: open vest and narrow sash must replace chef coat/toque/broad apron mass; the prop remains shallow and close to the waist.
- Courier: the tray is a compact horizontal waist-level gesture, not a parcel/satchel loadout.
- Usher: no long formal coat, peaked cap or brass-trimmed uniform column.

**Open design question:** confirm the tray reads as a shallow supported market tray, not a generic bowl, at direct 28×42 scale.

### 3. Clinic Worker — healthcare role

**Personality:** calm, attentive, reassuring and capable; clinical without looking like a generic lab scientist.

**Silhouette construction**
- Swept-back hair gathered into a compact low rear bun/coil; a small side tie sits at the gather. The hair mass, not a narrow headband, defines the head from back/profile.
- Short wrap-front clinical tunic with diagonal overlapping closure, shaped sides and softly curved hem.
- Visible trouser break below the tunic; no long coat.
- Relaxed attentive posture with one hand near a compact hip-mounted care pouch. No clipboard or serving pose.
- Rear includes a deliberate back-yoke/shoulder contour, gathered hair and curved tunic hem.

**Face construction cues**
- Front: softer tapered jaw, wider-set eye geometry and gently raised inner brow.
- Profile: shallower nose bridge and rounder chin than Guard; forehead-to-nose transition remains visible.
- Back: low gathered hair coil and side tie are the head-read.

**Exactly three must-survive items**
1. Swept-back gathered hair with low rear bun/coil (**28×42 head-read cue: rear hair gather and side tie**).
2. Short asymmetric wrap-front tunic with curved hem and visible trouser break.
3. Compact integrated care pouch/strap at the hip, subordinate to the body silhouette.

**Prop/contact and handedness**
- Primary proposal: compact care pouch attached to a short belt/waist strap on character-left hip (viewer-right in front; viewer-left in rear).
- This is a fixed wearable prop; no handheld medical kit is required in this exploration.
- The near hand can rest against the pouch edge, but must not fuse into it. Keep a visible wrist-to-palm transition and clear pouch/forearm occlusion.
- Profiles must show the pouch on the correct near/far hip and preserve the tunic overlap.

**28×42 simplification**
- Preserve low hair gather, wrap diagonal, curved tunic hem, trouser break and pouch block.
- Fine wrap stitching, pouch contents, badge text and individual hair strands may disappear.

**Closest roster comparisons to perform in the owner review**
- Usher: short curved tunic versus long formal coat.
- Cook: diagonal wrap and shaped side seams versus broad apron-front.
- Tech/Hacker: gathered hair and short tunic must remain distinct from generic technical/lab archetypes.

**Open design question:** ensure the gathered bun/side tie remains distinct from a generic hair bun when reduced to 28×42.
